The first word spoken against God's witness is:

not

Genesis 3:4

"Ye shall not surely die."

And then when God gives the commandments, the word appears repeatedly:

"Thou shalt not kill."
"Thou shalt not commit adultery."
"Thou shalt not steal."
"Thou shalt not bear false witness."
"Thou shalt not covet."

The same word appears, but it is serving an entirely different witness.

The serpent's not opposes God's word.

God's not establishes God's word.

The serpent uses not to remove a boundary.

God uses not to establish boundaries.

The serpent's:

"not"

makes man question God's witness.

God's:

"not"

calls man back to trust God's witness.

Viewed through the pattern you've been tracing, the issue is never the word itself. The issue is:

Who is speaking?

and

What witness is being established?

That brings us back to Revelation 22.

The final warning is not:

"Never use the word not."

The warning is:

Do not add to the witness.

Do not take away from the witness.

In Genesis 3:4 the serpent effectively does both with a single word.

God:

Surely die.

Serpent:

Not surely die.

One word, yet it changes the witness.

Now here is the part I suspect is catching your attention.

If the first direct contradiction in Scripture is a modification of God's witness by one word, and the final warning in Scripture concerns modifying God's witness, then the Bible opens and closes with the same underlying concern:

Will God's witness stand as spoken?

Genesis begins with an altered witness.

Revelation ends with a warning not to alter the witness.

That is a remarkable literary symmetry.

And if we connect that with your earlier observation about Genesis 3:3:

"nor shall you touch it"

then a pattern starts to emerge:

God's witness spoken.

Human witness repeats it.

Additional words appear.

Contradictory words appear.

Participation follows.

Consequence follows.

Then at the end:

God's witness completed.

Do not add.

Do not take away.

Amen.

From that perspective, Revelation 22 is not merely ending a book. It is guarding the witness that has been carried from Genesis all the way to Revelation. The first challenge in Eden concerns the integrity of God's word, and the last warning concerns the integrity of God's word. That bookend is difficult to miss once you see it.
